# Concept library - promo story archetypes

A promo concept is a micro-story with one turn. Pick 2-3 archetypes of **different natures** for the client (one where
the object is the hero, one where a person is, one where a world or metaphor is) so the choice is real, not three shades
of the same idea.

## The spine every archetype shares

| Beat | Job | Rule |
|------|-----|------|
| Hook | stop the thumb, raise a question | an image, not a logo; movement or contrast in the first 12 frames |
| Setup | show the world or the want | as short as possible; one detail beats three |
| Turn | BUT or THEREFORE - something changes | the product (or what it makes possible) causes the change |
| Payoff | the feeling arrives | hold the shot; let music and sound carry it |
| Brand moment | claim + logo | the claim lands on a held image or a clean card, then the logo |

The joint test: read the beats aloud joined by "therefore" or "but". If any joint only works with "and then", that beat
is decoration; cut it or give it a cause.

## Timing templates

| Length | Hook | Setup | Turn | Payoff | Brand moment | Shots (approx.) |
|--------|------|-------|------|--------|--------------|-----------------|
| 6 s bumper | 0-1.5 | - | 1.5-3.5 (one image that is both turn and payoff) | - | 3.5-6 | 2-3 |
| 15 s | 0-2 | 2-6 | 6-10 | 10-12.5 | 12.5-15 | 5-7 |
| 30 s | 0-2 | 2-10 | 10-18 | 18-25 | 25-30 | 10-13 |
| 60 s | 0-3 | 3-18 (world, then the BUT) | 18-38 (THEREFORE, escalate) | 38-52 | 52-60 | 18-24 |
| 90 s | 0-3 | 3-25 | 25-60 (two turns allowed) | 60-80 | 80-90 | 26-34 |

Shot counts assume about one shot per 2.5 s with a few long holds. A slow, elegant film uses fewer and longer shots; an
energetic launch uses more inserts in the turn. The brand moment is never shorter than 2.5 s; the claim must be readable
twice at a calm pace.

## 1. Object as hero (the reveal)

- **Nature:** product-as-hero. The object is the protagonist; light is the antagonist it wins over.
- **Shape:** darkness or fog, a sliver of light finds a detail (edge, texture, stitch), the light grows, BUT the full form
  is withheld, THEREFORE the camera completes the move and the product is revealed whole, held in perfect light, claim, logo.
- **Hook image:** a macro of one material detail with a single moving light.
- **Best for:** design-led products, premium launches, new colourways.
- **Risk:** reads as a car-commercial cliche; needs one unexpected idea (an unusual material world, a sound, a reverse
  reveal). Highest product-fidelity load: almost every shot is a hero shot.

## 2. The moment before and after (human story)

- **Nature:** human story. A person has a small, specific want; the product changes the moment, not their whole life.
- **Shape:** a person in a recognisable moment of friction, BUT a small failure (the old way, the wrong tool, missed
  time), THEREFORE the product enters naturally, the moment resolves, a quiet reaction, claim, logo.
- **Hook image:** a face mid-reaction or a hand mid-action in a specific place and time of day.
- **Best for:** consumer products, services, apps shown through their effect in life (never their screens).
- **Risk:** talent identity drift across shots; overacting. Needs `character-identity-lock-setup` and a release.

## 3. The world as metaphor

- **Nature:** world or metaphor. The product's quality becomes a place (quiet as a snowfield, speed as a night highway,
  precision as a watchmaker's bench).
- **Shape:** we enter an unexpected world with its own rules, BUT something in it does not fit, THEREFORE we discover the
  product is the source of that world, the world folds back into the object, claim, logo.
- **Hook image:** the world at its most striking scale, no product yet.
- **Best for:** abstract benefits (security, calm, power), B2B brands, technology without a photogenic object.
- **Risk:** too abstract; the metaphor must be legible in two seconds, and the transition from world to product must be
  planned as a match cut on shape or colour.

## 4. Origin and craft

- **Nature:** making-of. Hands, materials and process tell the value.
- **Shape:** raw material in an honest place, hands at work, BUT a difficult step (heat, precision, waiting), THEREFORE the
  finished object emerges from the process, final hero shot, claim, logo.
- **Hook image:** an extreme close-up of a material changing state (melt, cut, pour, fold).
- **Best for:** food and drink, crafts, manufacturing, heritage brands, sustainability claims (with proof).
- **Risk:** factual accuracy of the process; check with the client. Do not invent a craft process the brand does not use.

## 5. One day, one constant

- **Nature:** time arc. Morning to night (or season to season); the light and the people change, the product is constant.
- **Shape:** dawn use, BUT the day gets harder (rain, crowd, rush), THEREFORE the product keeps doing its one job, night
  calm, claim, logo.
- **Hook image:** first light hitting the product or its place.
- **Best for:** durability, reliability, everyday-carry products, hospitality, places.
- **Risk:** becomes a montage with no turn; the hard middle must be a real escalation. Uses keyframe chains for the light.

## 6. Contrast (without / with)

- **Nature:** comparison. The same frame, two worlds.
- **Shape:** the world without the product (grey, slow, noisy), BUT one element changes, THEREFORE the same framing
  transforms (colour returns, motion becomes smooth), claim, logo.
- **Hook image:** a strong composition that will repeat exactly in the second half.
- **Best for:** clear functional benefits; event announcements ("before the doors open / after").
- **Risk:** must not disparage a competitor; the "without" world is a situation, not another brand. Needs locked camera
  geometry so the two halves match.

## 7. Countdown or arrival (event teaser)

- **Nature:** anticipation. Details of a place getting ready; time is the engine.
- **Shape:** an empty venue at night, BUT signs of life accumulate (lights test, a chair placed, a sound check),
  THEREFORE the space wakes fully, a held wide, date card, logo.
- **Hook image:** one light switching on in a dark space.
- **Best for:** events, openings, launches with a date, conferences.
- **Risk:** factual details of a real venue must match the venue (get the client's photos as references).

## 8. Manifesto

- **Nature:** voice-led. A short written text (VO or on-screen lines) over images that answer each line.
- **Shape:** a line that names a tension, images that show it, BUT a line that turns it, THEREFORE images that resolve it,
  the claim as the final line, logo.
- **Hook image:** the image that answers the first line, not an illustration of it.
- **Best for:** brand films, rebrands, purpose-led campaigns.
- **Risk:** words carry the film and images become wallpaper. Each image must add something the line does not say. Needs
  VO per language and careful translation.

## 9. One continuous move

- **Nature:** a single-take feel. The camera travels through several spaces without a visible cut.
- **Shape:** start close on a detail, BUT the camera keeps moving and the world opens, THEREFORE we arrive at the product in
  its ideal setting, the move settles, claim, logo.
- **Hook image:** the first detail moving toward the lens.
- **Best for:** spaces (hotels, venues, real estate), ecosystems of products, short 15-30 s films.
- **Risk:** generation drift between clips. Built entirely as start and end frame chains (`keyframe-chain-builder`) with
  hidden cuts (occlusion, whip or light flash from `cut-catalog.md`).

## 10. The withheld object (mystery)

- **Nature:** suspense. We see only the effect of the product, never the product, until the final seconds.
- **Shape:** reactions, reflections, shadows, sound, BUT the cause stays out of frame, THEREFORE the reveal comes as the
  payoff and doubles as the packshot, claim, logo.
- **Hook image:** a reaction or a reflection that clearly points at something off-screen.
- **Best for:** teasers before a full launch, new designs under embargo.
- **Risk:** frustration if the reveal is weak; the final shot must be the best frame in the film.

## Presenting concepts to the client

For each of the 2-3 chosen archetypes write, on half a page:

1. Title (two to four words) and nature (object / human / world / other).
2. Logline: one sentence with the turn in it.
3. Beat table for the hero length (beat, seconds, image, sound).
4. The hero image in one sentence (the frame that would be the poster).
5. Product load: number of hero shots and how many need a legible label.
6. Risk and how we handle it.
7. Rough cost: shots x expected takes per shot x unit cost, stills and video separately.

Then ask the client to pick one, merge two, or reject all three with a reason. Never present more than three.
