# Visual story — Step 5: Exposition / establishing shots

Before covering a scene with character shots, every location needs its **establishing (exposition)
shot** — the wide frame that shows the space, its scale, palette, light, and era, and orients the
viewer. This is the shot that opens a scene and "exposes" the world. It's a distinct deliverable
between the reference bible and scene production: the reference sheets define what a location IS; the
establishing shot is the first real in-story frame of it, and it becomes that scene's **master**.

## What to produce

For each location the script visits, one (or a few) **wide establishing shot(s)**:
- Wide / extreme-wide framing that reveals the whole space and its composition (symmetry, depth,
  color-blocking — whatever the project's style dictates).
- Built from the location's approved **location sheet** (attach it) so the architecture matches, and
  obeying the **STYLE LOCK block** so palette/light/grain match the rest of the film.
- Include the atmosphere the location carries (haze, weather, time of day) — this is what every
  character entering the scene must then match (continuity/weather propagation, handled in step 6).
- Usually empty or near-empty of the main characters (it establishes the place, not the action) —
  though a small distant figure for scale is fine if the style uses it.

## Why it's its own step

- **It's the location master.** The approved establishing shot is attached as a reference to every
  subsequent shot in that scene, so the room/street never gets reinvented across coverage.
- **It sets the exposition rhythm.** Scenes generally open on the establishing wide, then cut in —
  having these first makes production (step 6) a matter of cutting in, not re-establishing.
- **It locks atmosphere.** Weather, light, time of day are decided here and propagate to every
  character shot in the scene.

## Order

1. For each location, take its locked location sheet + the STYLE LOCK block.
2. Generate the wide establishing shot; QC it against the sheet (same architecture) and the style
   (same palette/light/grain).
3. Approve it — it's now the scene's master. Save it into that scene's working folder AND note it as
   the location's establishing/master frame.

## When done

Every location has an approved establishing shot / master frame. **Next: `visual-story-6-production` —
cover each scene by cutting in from these establishing shots, attaching them as masters.**
