# Worked example: "The Tuner", chapter 1

An original short story invented for this skill, worked end to end: chapter card, three scene cards with every
joint, hand-offs between scenes, the ledger rows, then an audit of a flawed draft paragraph and its fix. The
screen-bridge version of scene 1.1 is in `screen-bridge.md`. The prose here is English; for a book in another
language the cards stay exactly as they are and only the prose changes language.

**Premise.** Ines Varga, a freelance piano tuner in an old city, works through a tuning shop owned by Ruth Dorn.
Her rent is due Friday at 8 pm and she does not have it. Ines believes a piano is right when it matches the
standard. The book will teach her that people want *their* sound.

Book circle (context only): Ines wants to be the best tuner in the city by the standard; she needs to learn to
tune for the person, not the fork. This chapter carries book beats 1 to 3.

## Scene 1.1 card

```
SCENE 1.1 - The Flat Piano
Chapter: 1  |  Owner: Ines  |  Place / time: Hale's flat, Thursday afternoon
Point (one sentence): a perfectionist has to break her own work to get paid.
Chapter beat carried: 1, 2, 3
Hand-off in: (chapter opening)

1. YOU ........ Ines tunes her eighth piano of the week, fast and flawless, striking her fork
                on her knee before every string; the landlord's final notice folded in her case.
        | BUT
2. NEED ....... Hale, who booked a standard tuning, says he will pay only when it sounds
                "like Clara's" (his late wife). She needs the fee from this room, today.
        | THEREFORE
3. GO ......... Instead of leaving with nothing, she accepts. Tactic: tune it perfectly and trust
                that perfect will sound right. Going costs her next appointment; she cancels it.
        | BUT
4. SEARCH ..... A1 perfect A440 - BUT "a shop piano, bright".
                A2 the old pitch, A435, forty minutes - BUT the wallet goes back in the drawer.
                A3 she asks him to hum Clara's evening piece; on the fourth note he goes sour,
                twice, in the same place - THEREFORE she lifts the lid and finds a pencil card
                between the pins: "B flat. Leave it. H. likes it."
        | THEREFORE
5. FIND ....... She knows how to make it "right" - but right means putting one string out of
                tune on purpose. He never wanted a tuned piano; he wanted the flaw.
        | BUT
6. TAKE ....... The shop's service sheet must say "tuned to standard pitch". She detunes the
                B flat, signs the false sheet with her full name, and presses the shop's
                guarantee sticker into the lid. Her signature is on a lie the shop can check.
        | THEREFORE
7. RETURN ..... Hale plays, laughs out loud on the sour note, pays in cash, more than agreed,
                and gives her Clara's card: "You'll need it next time."
        | THEREFORE
8. CHANGE ..... On the landing she lifts her tuning fork to check her ear, as after every job,
                and puts it back without striking it.

HAND-OFF OUT: BUT -> need of 1.2: Hale phones the shop to thank them "for leaving the B flat
              alone"; the guarantee is void; Ruth wants it fixed or Ines is out.

Partial circles in the scene:
  Hale - beats 2 and 6: wants to hear the evening piece as it was; pays by giving away
         the last thing in his wife's hand.

Prose notes: POV close third, Ines | tense past | enter at beat 1 inside the work | leave at beat 8
Threads: opens T01 (Clara's card), T02 (sticker in the lid), T03 (rent)

Checks:
  [x] one owner             [x] find != need            [x] price cannot be restored
  [x] change is shown       [x] no "and then" joint     [x] point not shared with another scene
  [x] hand-off in and out   [x] beats 3-6 carry ~2/3 of the words
```

Rhythm: BUT, THEREFORE, BUT, THEREFORE, BUT, THEREFORE, THEREFORE. Four THEREFORE, three BUT, no run longer than
two. Symmetry check: 1 and 8 rhyme (the fork struck / the fork not struck); 2 and 6 rhyme (she wants his money /
she pays with her signature to get it).

## Scene 1.2 card

```
SCENE 1.2 - The Guarantee
Chapter: 1  |  Owner: Ines  |  Place / time: Dorn's tuning shop, Friday 9 am
Point (one sentence): telling the truth costs more than the lie did.
Chapter beat carried: 4, 5
Hand-off in: BUT from 1.1 beat 8: Hale's thank-you call voided the guarantee.

1. YOU ........ Ines at the counter at opening, Hale's cash for the rent buttoned inside her coat,
                waiting for her weekly job list.
        | BUT
2. NEED ....... Ruth hands her no list: the guarantee is void. Ines must keep the contract
                without going back to "fix" Hale's piano.
        | THEREFORE
3. GO ......... She lies: Hale is confused, the piano is at standard.
        | BUT
4. SEARCH ..... A1 the lie - BUT Ruth calls Hale on speaker and he cheerfully confirms the B flat.
                A2 "the client asked for it" - BUT Ruth taps the sticker on the job photo: the
                shop's name guarantees standard, and the next tuner will see it.
                A3 she offers to pay the shop's cut and the guarantee fee from her own fee -
                THEREFORE Ruth opens the cash drawer.
        | THEREFORE
5. FIND ....... Ruth keeps her - as second tuner behind Tomas, the apprentice Ines trained.
                His signature goes on the sheets now, not hers.
        | BUT
6. TAKE ....... The cut and the fee come to almost all of the rent. She counts it out on the
                counter. Rent is due tonight.
        | THEREFORE
7. RETURN ..... She walks out with the job list, her name typed under Tomas's.
        | THEREFORE
8. CHANGE ..... At the door she takes the roll of shop guarantee stickers out of her kit and
                leaves it on the counter.

HAND-OFF OUT: THEREFORE -> need of 1.3: find the rent by 8 pm, on her own.

Partial circles in the scene:
  Ruth - beats 2 and 6: protect the shop's name (her father's); loses her best tuner's signature.
  Tomas - beat 2 only: wants the senior slot.

Threads: T02 stays open (the sticker is still in Hale's piano: due chapter 2)
```

## Scene 1.3 card

```
SCENE 1.3 - Three Pianos
Chapter: 1  |  Owner: Ines  |  Place / time: Hale's building, Friday 6 pm
Point (one sentence): the city has more people like Hale than the shop knows.
Chapter beat carried: 6, 7, 8
Hand-off in: THEREFORE from 1.2 beat 8: she has walked away from the shop's stickers and has no rent.

1. YOU ........ Ines on Hale's landing with a tenth of the rent, rehearsing how to ask an old man
                for a loan.
        | THEREFORE
2. NEED ....... Get the rent by 8 pm: ask Hale for an advance on future work.
        | THEREFORE
3. GO ......... She knocks.
        | BUT
4. SEARCH ..... A1 Hale is not alone: three neighbours over tea, each with a piano "nobody gets
                right"; she cannot ask for money in front of them.
                BUT A2 they want her on Saturday, the morning Ruth has her booked behind Tomas.
                BUT A3 she asks for payment in advance; they hesitate until Hale puts Clara's card
                on the table: "She'll do what Clara did."
        | THEREFORE
5. FIND ....... They pay in advance, enough for the rent: for work the shop forbids, on the
                shop's day.
        | BUT
6. TAKE ....... Ruth texts: "Saturday 8 am or don't come back." Ines answers that she will not
                come. The shop is gone for good (the chapter's price).
        | THEREFORE
7. RETURN ..... She runs; at 7:52 she counts the notes out on the landlord's doormat.
        | THEREFORE
8. CHANGE ..... At her kitchen table she writes a new card for her kit, "Tuned to memory, not to
                standard", and slips Clara's card behind it.

HAND-OFF OUT (chapter): BUT -> Ruth sends Tomas to retune Hale's piano "to standard" on Monday.

Threads: pays off T01 (Clara's card becomes her credential), T03 (rent paid)
```

Rhythm note: this scene opens THEREFORE, THEREFORE because its need was already handed in by 1.2; the search
then runs three BUTs. That is acceptable (no run longer than two at the beat level), and it mirrors the chapter's
turn: Ines is choosing now, not only being pushed.

## Audit of a flawed draft

The writer's first draft of scene 1.1, beats 4 to 8, came in as one paragraph:

> Ines tuned the A to 440 and then she played a chord. Mr. Hale said it was too bright. After that she lowered
> the pitch to 435. He still did not like it. Then she opened the lid and suddenly she found a card. She
> realized that it was Clara's handwriting and that Clara had always left the B flat out of tune. She felt sad
> for him. She detuned the string and signed the sheet. It was clear that he was happy. Ines realized she was
> no longer the tuner she had been that morning.

Linter output (`python3 scripts/prose_lint.py flawed.md`, trimmed):

```
== Weak sequence connectors (3)
  L1:25   high   and_then      "and then"   Ines tuned the A to 440 and then she played a chord...
  L1:87   high   after_that    "After that" ...said it was too bright. After that she lowered the pitch...
  L1:154  medium then_opening  "Then"       ...did not like it. Then she opened the lid and suddenly...
== Telling instead of showing (6)
  L1:182  low    suddenly      "suddenly"
  L1:209  medium realized      "She realized that"
  L1:309  medium felt          "She felt"
  L1:376  medium it_was_clear  "It was clear"
  L1:394  medium named_emotion "he was happy"
  L1:413  medium realized      "realized"
Summary: 9 finding(s): high 2, medium 6, low 1.
```

The audit report:

```
AUDIT: scene 1.1, beats 4-8 (draft 1), 94 words, language: English

-- WHAT IS THERE -----------------------------------
4. SEARCH ..... A1 "tuned the A to 440" | A2 "lowered the pitch to 435" | A3 MISSING
5. FIND ....... "realized that it was Clara's handwriting" (told, not found)
6. TAKE ....... "detuned the string and signed the sheet" (summarized in eight words)
7. RETURN ..... "It was clear that he was happy" (verdict, no action)
8. CHANGE ..... "no longer the tuner she had been" (report of a change, not a change)

-- CHAIN -------------------------------------------
A1 --BUT (hidden behind "After that")--> A2 --AND THEN (dead)--> card --THEREFORE--> 5
   --AND THEN (dead)--> 6 --AND THEN (dead)--> 7 --AND THEN (dead)--> 8

-- LINTER ------------------------------------------
9 hits. "and then" (L1:25) is harmless: it joins two actions inside one attempt.
"After that" (L1:87) hides a real BUT: keep the logic, make it visible.
"Then ... suddenly she found a card" (L1:154) is the real dead joint: nothing makes her open the lid.
All six telling hits are real.

-- DIAGNOSIS (most severe first) -------------------
1. Dead joint A2 -> card: the card is found by luck, so the find is not earned (failure mode 1).
2. Price summarized: the false signature, the whole cost of the scene, takes eight words (mode 3 symptoms:
   the reader never feels it).
3. Change told: "no longer the tuner she had been" (mode 6).
4. Find told: "she realized" hands the reader the conclusion (prose check: show, don't tell).
5. Return told by verdict: "it was clear that he was happy".

-- FIXES -------------------------------------------
1. Add attempt 3 as a tactic grown from failure 2: she asks Hale to hum the piece; his sour note
   makes her look inside the lid. The joint becomes THEREFORE.
2. Dramatize the price: the lever turns, the string goes off; the sheet says "tuned to standard
   pitch"; her pen stops halfway through her surname; the shop's sticker goes in next to the card.
3. Replace the summary of the change with the fork: lifted over her knee, put back unstruck
   (rhymes with beat 1, where she strikes it before every string).
4. Let the card speak for itself (its pencil text), cut "she realized".
5. Show Hale's joy as an action (a laugh on the sour note, cash, the card given away).

-- WHAT WORKS (leave alone) ------------------------
The two failed attempts in the right order (bright standard, then old pitch). The card in the lid
as the hinge of the scene. The detuning as the act that breaks her rule.
```

## The fix

```markdown
<!-- B4 -->
She set the A to 440 and played Clara's evening piece from the sheet on the stand, the one with the corners gone
soft from thumbs. Hale listened with his eyes shut.

"A shop piano," he said. "Bright. She never had it bright."

So she brought the whole instrument down to the old pitch, four hundred and thirty-five, string by string, forty
minutes of it. He listened again. He took his wallet out of his cardigan, looked at it, and put it back in the
sideboard drawer.

"Hum it," Ines said. "The way she played it."

He cleared his throat twice and hummed. On the fourth note his voice dropped, sour and certain, and came back
up. He did it again in the same place.

She lifted the lid. Tucked between the tuning pins, where only a tuner would look, was a card in pencil:
*B flat. Leave it. H. likes it.*

<!-- B5 -->
She could make it right in two minutes. She had never in her life made a string wrong on purpose.

<!-- B6 -->
She fitted the lever, turned it a hair to the left, and listened to the B flat go off like milk. Her service
sheet lay on the music stand: *Tuned to standard pitch.* Her pen stopped halfway through her surname. She
finished it fast and pressed the shop's guarantee sticker inside the lid, next to Clara's card.

<!-- B7 -->
Hale played. On the fourth note he laughed out loud, a short bark, and paid her in cash, more than they had
agreed. At the door he pressed Clara's card into her hand. "You'll need it next time."

<!-- B8 -->
On the landing she took out her tuning fork, as she did after every job, and held it over her knee. She put it
back in the case without striking it.
```

Linter on the fix: no connector or telling hits; beat weight B4 51 %, B5 7 %, B6 19 %, B7 13 %, B8 10 %
(beats 3-6 at 77 %); one low note, "no words under beats 1, 2, 3", expected because this is an excerpt.

What changed, joint by joint: A1 --BUT--> A2 --BUT--> A3 --THEREFORE--> card --THEREFORE--> 5 --BUT--> 6
--THEREFORE--> 7 --THEREFORE--> 8. Every dead joint is now causal, the price is on the page, and the change is an
object the reader can see.
