# Diagnostics

Structural failure modes, the stuck ladder and the 5-minute chapter check. Sentence-level problems (POV, tense,
telling, dialogue) live in `prose-checks.md`.

## Scene failure modes

Go top to bottom. The first four are heavy: they kill a scene. The rest are fixed with an edit.

**1. "And then" chain.** No causality between beats.
-> Change beats, not joints. See `causality.md`.

**2. Flat circle.** The scene ends where it began: there is no beat 8. The owner leaves the scene as they entered
it, and nobody next to them changed either.
-> Add a visible change, or hand the change to a second character (the antihero rule in `circle.md`), or admit the
scene is a service scene and merge it with a neighbour.

**3. Cheap price.** Beat 6 can be undone: a bruise, lost time, a spoiled object. Harmon: it cannot be merely bad,
it has to be devastating.
-> Ask: what does the hero already have that they could never get back? Take that.

**4. Find equals need.** They got exactly what they wanted, exactly the way they wanted it. Wish fulfilment, not
story.
-> Distort the find: wrong form, wrong price, wrong moment, from the wrong person.

**5. Two owners.** Two characters drive the scene equally; the reader does not know whose story this is.
-> Pick one. Leave the other a partial circle (beats 2 and 6 are enough).

**6. Change told, not shown.** "He realized that...", "from that day on she was different".
-> Replace with an action, an object, a refusal, a line. Change has to be made visual.

**7. Empty search.** Beat 4 is a montage or a conversation without failures.
-> Three attempts, each cut off by its own BUT, each leaving the hero something.

**8. Win without a shift.** The hero wins at beat 7 although nothing shifted inside between 4 and 5.
-> Either write the inner shift in, or take the win away.

**9. Duplicate point.** Two scenes make the same statement.
-> Merge, or cut the weaker.

**10. Weight skew.** Half the text sits in beats 1-2.
-> Start the scene later. Rule: enter as close to beat 3 as you can. The linter flags this when beat markers are
present.

**11. Tactical need replaced by the life-want.** Beat 2 says "wants redemption".
-> Bring it down to scene level: what exactly must they get in these pages?

**12. Antagonist without a circle.** They have neither a want nor a price.
-> Give them at least beats 2 and 6.

The four heavy modes (1 to 4) map onto the hard rules in `SKILL.md`. Modes 6 and 10 also have sentence-level
symptoms the linter can surface; the diagnosis still comes from reading.

## The stuck ladder (Mode D)

When the writer says "I don't know what happens next" or "the scene won't move", check in this order. Stop at
the first rung that works.

1. **Which beat are you on?** In most cases the writer is stuck in beat 4 and cannot get to 5 because they have
   not decided how the find will be distorted. Decide the distortion and the way out appears.
2. **Is there a price?** If beat 6 is not in the plan, there is nothing to write toward: the scene has nowhere to
   fall. Set the price before going back to the text.
3. **Check the joint backwards.** Is the connector from the previous scene into this one "and then"? Then this
   scene has no reason to begin. Fix the previous scene, not this one.
4. **Whose scene is it?** If the owner is not named, or there are two, the text will sprawl however many times it
   is rewritten.
5. **What does the hero choose here?** If they only react, the chain is all BUT. Give them a choice that a
   THEREFORE can grow from.
6. **Sketch test.** State the point of the scene in one sentence. If it will not come, the scene is not needed:
   deal its contents out to its neighbours.
7. **Start later, finish earlier.** Cut the entrance to just before beat 3 and the exit to just after beat 8.
8. **The book circle.** If nothing helps, the scene probably does not move any beat of the book circle. Then the
   question is not "how to write it" but "why".

Offer the writer two or three concrete options at the rung that fires (for example three possible distortions of
the find); the writer chooses.

## The 5-minute chapter check

- Does every scene in the chapter have an owner?
- Are the connectors between scenes written down, and is none of them "and then"?
- Is the chapter's price (beat 6) bigger than any single scene's price inside it?
- Does the chapter's change produce the need of the next chapter?
- Does any scene duplicate another scene's point?
- Do beats 3-6 of the chapter take about two thirds of its length?
- Are the threads due in this chapter (see `BOOK_LEDGER.md`) paid off, or explicitly moved?
